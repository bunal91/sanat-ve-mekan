## Başlık

**Eggshell–Agar Biocomposites for Interior Surfaces: A Production Protocol and
Exploratory Characterisation**

Alternatifler:
- *From Local Waste Stream to Interior Surface: An Open Production Protocol for
  Eggshell–Agar Biocomposites*
- *Documenting Designer-Led Biofabrication: A Reproducible Protocol for an
  Eggshell-Based Biocomposite*

## Abstract (≈290 kelime)

Eggshell waste is generated in large volumes worldwide, yet its valorisation in
design and construction remains dominated by its use as a mineral filler within
polymer or cement matrices. This paper reports a fully bio-based alternative: a
biocomposite produced from waste eggshell powder, agar, glycerol and water,
developed through an iterative process spanning more than a year, using
eggshells collected from a single local patisserie.

The primary contribution is a documented, reproducible production protocol —
component selection rationale, sterilisation and milling of the shell fraction,
preparation sequence, casting and drying parameters, and a formulation matrix.
This responds to a specific gap: the absence of shared protocols currently
confines designer-led biofabrication to trial and error, and prevents results
from different studies being compared at all.

Alongside the protocol, exploratory characterisation is reported: compression,
three-point flexure (ASTM D790), single-flame ignitability (ISO 11925-1) and
water absorption (ASTM D570). These tests were conducted on single specimens per
formulation and are presented as preliminary indications rather than validated
material properties. Within these limits, two formulation–behaviour
relationships were consistent. Reducing water content while increasing eggshell
and agar fractions raised load-bearing capacity. Halving the glycerol content
shifted the material from an unmeasurably ductile state to a measurable flexural
response (σf = 2.33 MPa, Ef = 192 MPa). The material self-extinguished within
four seconds of flame removal, and absorbed 0.7% of its mass over 24 hours of
immersion when shellac-coated.

The discussion argues that the material's low stiffness — conventionally
reported as a deficiency — yields a modulus of resilience competitive with
brittle mineral interior materials, and proposes resilience rather than strength
as the appropriate evaluation criterion for bio-based materials. The paper
positions designer-led biofabrication as an open research field in which shared
protocols must precede comparative characterisation.

**Keywords:** eggshell waste, biocomposite, biofabrication protocol, agar,
material resilience, circular design

  olduğu açıkça yazılacak. (bkz. `../05-veri/tez-veri-dokumu.md` §2)

  aralık olarak verilecek.

  (Hacettepe University, 2025)." — dipnot olarak.
