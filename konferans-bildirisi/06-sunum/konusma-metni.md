## 1 · Cover — 0:15

Good morning. Betül Ünal, Department of Interior Architecture, Cappadocia
University. The panel in the photograph is the subject of this paper: a
composite of waste eggshell bound in agar.

---

## 2 · Background — 0:30

Eggshell is produced continuously as a by-product of food processing, and almost
all of it goes to landfill. Chemically it is very close to pure calcium
carbonate, and its specific gravity is lower than that of most mineral fillers
used in construction.

[Sağdaki şemaya işaret et]

It therefore has two possible routes: landfill, or use as a secondary mineral
resource. **The case for eggshell as a mineral phase is settled. This paper is
about the phase that binds it.**

---

## 3 · Prior applications — 0:30

This table is the literature the study departs from. Eggshell has already been
used in concrete, in brick and clay stabilisation, in asphalt, in ceramic tile,
in PVC and polyurethane foam, and even in bone scaffolds.

[Elini orta sütuna götür, aşağı indir]

Reading down the middle column, the pattern is consistent. In every case the
mineral is recovered from waste, but the binding phase remains cementitious,
ceramic or petrochemical. **The burden is carried by the binder, and the binder
is left untouched.**

---

## 4 · Research gap — 0:35

Three gaps follow. The first is that the binding phase is never biological:
**no study reports an eggshell composite in which both phases are of biological
origin.** The second concerns agar, which has been characterised only as an
unfilled film in tension, and **never as the binder of a mineral-filled solid in
bending.** The third is that **property data is scattered across separate
studies on separate formulations.**

**This is not a novel constituent. It is a documented protocol and a single
coherent property set.**

---

## 5 · Aim — 0:20

The aim is deliberately narrow: **to document a reproducible production protocol
for a fully bio-based eggshell–agar composite, and to characterise it against
published test standards.** Four objectives follow, and **the fourth objective
is the one the paper turns on.**

---

## 6 · Scope — 0:25

The boundaries of the work are set out here, so that nothing said today is read
beyond them. Five formulations were produced, with three specimens each for the
mechanical tests, and three published standards were applied. The whole series
was cured at ambient conditions in a single laboratory, and the application
envelope throughout is non-loadbearing interior surfaces.

[Alttaki şeride işaret et]

**The filled boxes were determined in this study. The outlined boxes were not.**

---

## 7 · Method — 0:20

The method has four stages, and the order matters. The material was produced
with every parameter recorded, measured against published standards, positioned
against two material families, and only then was a criterion for evaluating it
proposed. **The properties came first, and the application envelope was derived
from them.**

---

## 8 · Research questions — 0:15

Five questions. The first four concern formulation and benchmarking; the fifth
asks whether compressive strength is an adequate criterion for this class of
material at all. **The first four are answered by measurement. The fifth is
answered by argument.**

---

## 9 · Hypothesis — 0:25

The hypothesis reads as follows. **A fully bio-based eggshell–agar composite can
be produced repeatably under ambient conditions, and its performance as an
interior surface material is governed by deformation capacity rather than by
compressive strength.** Those are two separate claims, tested by different parts
of the paper.

**The dissertation behind this paper frames a broader hypothesis around spatial
resilience; this is its materials-engineering component.**

---

## 10 · Stage 1 · Mineral phase — 0:25

The first stage prepares the mineral. The shell comes from a single bakery, and
it is cleaned by hand, heat-treated at one hundred and twenty degrees for twenty
minutes, milled, and sieved. **The 150 micrometre fraction was selected so that
the mineral phase would be comparable with the eggshell polymer-composite
literature.** **The heat step is decontamination and drying rather than
sterilisation, because sterility was never verified.**

---

## 11 · Stage 2 · Binder preparation — 0:35

The second stage prepares the binder. **Agar is thermoreversible: it melts on
heating and sets again on cooling, so no hardener and no cure temperature are
needed.** **The agar is added a little at a time under constant stirring,
because added all at once it forms agglomerates that never redisperse.** The
mixture is held at ninety-five degrees until fully dissolved, brought down to
eighty while the viscosity builds, and the shell goes in near the end. The whole
preparation takes fifty-five minutes.

---

## 12 · Stage 3 · Casting and cure — 0:25

The third stage is casting and curing. The mixture is cast into silicone moulds,
demoulded on the fourth day, and left for a further seven days at around
twenty-three degrees. **After the 55-minute preparation the material receives no
further heat, so the entire cure happens at room temperature.**

[Panele işaret et]

**The composite shrinks as the water evaporates, and the dried dimensions were
not logged, so every dimension reported here is a mould dimension.**

---

## 13 · Formulations — 0:30

Two things to notice in this matrix. In the compression series the
shell-to-binder ratio is exactly two in all three mixes, which means that
**solids concentration is the only variable, and the bars show it: the water
band gets shorter from C1 to C3.**

In the flexural series a confound has to be declared. **The glycerol was halved
while the batch mass stayed the same, so the water rose to compensate.**

---

## 14 · Test programme — 0:20

Four specimen geometries, with the standard and the number of specimens beneath
each. The reporting convention belongs on the record before any numbers appear.
**The mechanical values are means of three specimens, and since the individual
values were not retained, standard deviations cannot be reported. The fire,
absorption and density figures are single determinations.**

---

## 15 · Compression — 0:30

This answers the first question. Compressive stress rises linearly with solids
concentration, from two and a half to four megapascals. Three points will fit
almost any line, so this is offered as a hypothesis rather than a law. The
physical reading, though, is clear. **Strength here is governed by packing
density, not by mineral content. The way to a stronger composite is to take
water out, not to put more shell in.**

---

## 16 · Compressive failure — 0:30

The curve is linear to about one megapascal, and then the stiffness falls away
as the agar network yields and the mineral redistributes. The peak arrives at
around ten per cent strain, with no sudden drop in load.

[Fotoğrafı göster]

**The cube has barrelled: shorter, wider, and still a single body. Failure
proceeds by progressive crushing rather than by fracture.** **The strain scale
assumes ten per cent deformation from observation, not instrumented
measurement.**

---

## 17 · Flexure — 0:30

In bending the decisive variable is the plasticiser. At eighteen point eight per
cent glycerol the specimen deformed without developing any measurable
resistance; halving the glycerol gave two point three megapascals at a modulus
of one hundred and ninety-two. **The transition is sharp, and it lies between
those two compositions.**

**The specimen is kerfed on its tension face while the standard assumes a solid
rectangle, so the modulus reported here is a lower bound.**

---

## 18 · Flexural deformation — 0:15

The photographs make the point more directly than the numbers. **The beam is
deflected well past the point where a comparable ceramic would have parted, and
it is still intact. Both test programmes gave the same result: substantial
deformation with the integrity retained.**

---

## 19 · Fire, water, density — 0:30

The material ignites at around ten seconds under a direct flame and extinguishes
itself four seconds after the flame is removed, with no lateral spread and no
dripping. **It is an ignitability test, and there is no Euroclass classification
behind it.**

The mass gain over twenty-four hours of immersion was zero point seven per cent,
so **it is the coating that limits humid service rather than the composite
itself.**

---

## 20 · Conventional materials — 0:20

The first benchmark is against the materials this would sit beside in an
interior. **It has one sixth the compressive strength of MDF and one twentieth
that of travertine. On strength alone this composite is not competitive with
engineered wood or stone.**

---

## 21 · Bio-based composites — 0:30

The second benchmark is the one that matters. In compression the composite
stands an order of magnitude above mycelium and hempcrete, and in stiffness, at
one hundred and ninety-two megapascals, it sits inside the LDPE band.

[Logaritmik eksene işaret et]

**It was cast from water and dried at room temperature, with no oven, no
autoclave and no extruder, and it still reaches the stiffness of melt-processed
bio-polymers.**

---

## 22 · Modulus of resilience — 0:50

This brings the talk to the fifth question, and the diagram is the argument.

The shaded area is the energy a material absorbs per unit volume before
permanent damage. That area is one half sigma epsilon, and since epsilon is
sigma over E, it reduces to sigma squared over two E. **The modulus sits in the
denominator.** So the low stiffness that counted against the material a moment
ago is exactly what raises this quantity: about fourteen kilojoules per cubic
metre.

**Strength records the load at which a material stops performing. It does not
record whether failure is then graceful or catastrophic** — and for a
demountable component, that is what decides service life. **A comparative table
cannot be assembled, because the literature reports strength and modulus in
separate studies.**

---

## 23 · Application envelope — 0:20

The application envelope follows from the measurements: non-loadbearing
surfaces, dry interiors, away from ignition sources, and modular, so that damage
can be replaced one unit at a time. **No life-cycle assessment was carried out,
so these are qualitative potentials rather than quantified claims.**

---

## 24 · Limitations and further work — 0:25

Each limitation is placed beside the experiment that resolves it, because that
pairing is the point. There is no dispersion data, the strain was assumed rather
than instrumented, and the shrinkage was never quantified. **Every value
reported here is a lower bound.**

---

## 25 · Conclusions — 0:25

Five conclusions. The protocol can be repeated. Packing density, not mineral
content, governs compressive strength. Plasticiser content decides whether the
material is brittle or ductile. A water-cast, ambient-dried material can reach
bio-polymer stiffness. **And strength on its own is not an adequate criterion
for this class: strength and modulus should be reported together.**

Thank you.

---

# Soru–cevap

Burada birinci tekil şahıs doğal; soru sana sorulmuş oluyor. Kısa tut:
sınırlılığı kabul et, sonraki deneyi söyle, sus.

**"Standart sapma yok, nasıl güveneceğiz?"**
That is correct, and it was stated before any numbers were shown. There were
three specimens per formulation and the individual values were not retained.
Repeating the series with dispersion reported is the first row of the
limitations table.

**"Basma testi hangi standarda göre?"**
Cube compression rather than a named standard, and that is flagged as a
deviation. The flexural work follows ASTM D790-17, the ignitability test
ISO 11925-2, and the absorption test ASTM D570.

**"Ölçüler kalıp ölçüsü, gerçek gerilme değil."**
Correct. The composite shrinks as the water evaporates and the dried dimensions
were not logged, so every value reported is a lower bound. Recording the dried
geometry is in the limitations table.

**"Yivli numuneden modül hesaplanmaz."**
Agreed, which is why it was reported as a lower bound rather than a material
constant. Testing an un-kerfed section is in the table.

**"R² = 0.99 ama üç nokta var."**
Which is exactly why it was offered as a hypothesis rather than a law. The claim
is the physical reading: packing density, not mineral content, governs strength.

**"Neden agar? Nişasta ya da jelatin olmaz mıydı?"**
Agar sets thermoreversibly without a hardener, and it carried sixty-six per cent
mineral loading without a coupling agent. Comparing binders would be a separate
study, and a fair one to ask for.

**"Yangın mekanizması nedir?"**
The probable mechanism is endothermic decomposition of the carbonate fraction,
which dilutes the flame front. Probable, because it was not verified
calorimetrically.

**"Nem, küf, yaşlanma?"**
All untested, and named as such on the limitations slide. Durability of any kind
is outside the scope of this paper.

**"Ölçeklenebilir mi?"**
There is no data on that. What can be said is that the protocol needs a
hotplate, a mould and ambient drying, and nothing else.

**"Yangın sınıflandırması olmadan iç mekanda nasıl kullanılacak?"**
It cannot be specified without one, and the application slide says so. What is
reported is ignitability behaviour, which is the precondition for seeking a
classification rather than a substitute for it.
