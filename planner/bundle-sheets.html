Four sets,
one shelf.

Four separate planning sets, each designed on its own terms rather than recoloured from one template. You do not need all four. Most people use one, and borrow a page or two from another.

1
First MoveStarting, and time · 8 pages

One real task a day, a first move small enough to do, a brain dump with a column for letting things go, and a page that teaches you how long things really take.

2
Same ShapeOrder, and change · 9 pages

The day as a sequence, a page for when the plan changes, sensory needs written down once, and sentences prepared before you need them.

3
SteadyWorry, and evidence · 9 pages

A worry window with a closing time, a thought record, grounding, and a log of what you feared against what happened.

4
Small LightLow days, and safety · 9 pages

A daily page that takes two minutes, three versions of a day written in advance, an activity log, and a safety plan.

Please read this part. These are journals and planners. They are not therapy, treatment, diagnosis or medical care, and they do not replace them. If any of this is making daily life hard, talking to a doctor is worth more than any planner. If you are in danger or thinking about harming yourself, contact your local emergency number or a crisis line now. Small Light page 8 is where you write those numbers down — do that today, while you can.
1

Open the second sheet first

“Which page today” is a table: what is happening on the left, which set and page on the right. Print it and keep it at the front of the folder.

2

Print one daily page, not four

Pick the set that matches this month and print ten copies of its first page. Four daily pages is four decisions before breakfast.

3

Fill the written-when-calm pages early

Same Shape 4 and 5, Steady 9, Small Light 2 and 8 are written on a good day and used on a bad one. That is the whole point of them.
