# EXTENDED ENGLISH SUMMARY

**Selection of bio-based building envelope materials under the equivalent
thermal performance assumption: how the framing determines the result**

**Purpose.** Comparative studies of bio-based insulation materials commonly
adopt an equivalent thermal performance framing, in which every material is
applied at the thickness required to meet a common thermal transmittance
target, and implicitly assume that the resulting ranking varies with climate.
This study does not aim to identify a best material; it tests that framing
itself within the six degree-day zones introduced by TS 825:2024, which became
mandatory in Türkiye on 1 April 2025 and, for the first time, incorporates
cooling demand.

**Method.** Fourteen bio-based and four conventional alternatives were compared
using a functional unit of one square metre of external wall meeting the
recommended transmittance of the relevant zone. Criteria comprised thermal
conductivity, unit mass, areal heat capacity, life-cycle carbon, reaction to
fire, end-of-life scenario, moisture sensitivity and loss of wall thickness.
Weights were derived by Shannon entropy, CRITIC and equal weighting; rankings
by TOPSIS with VIKOR as a consistency check. Environmental data were taken from
verified product declarations, and climate data from an open European service.
The calculation basis was validated against a published minimum insulation
thickness table at eight data points, with exact agreement. Missing values were
not set to zero; criteria exceeding fifty per cent missingness were excluded and
reported.

**Result 1 — the framing cannot produce a climate-zone effect.** The required
insulation thickness equals the thermal conductivity multiplied by a factor that
depends only on the zone. Every criterion derived from thickness therefore
carries a common, material-independent factor, which cancels exactly in any
normalisation insensitive to column-wise scaling. With rounding disabled, the
inter-zone rank correlation is 1.000000 for three weighting methods and two
ranking methods, and the maximum difference between entropy weights across zones
is zero. All observed deviation stems from rounding thickness to the nearest
centimetre. A zone effect emerges only when structures that break this
proportionality are introduced: a buildability constraint eliminates four
bio-based alternatives in the coldest zones, and climate-dependent weighting
reduces the correlation from 0.998 to 0.567.

**Result 2 — the weighting method is directional.** Entropy assigns
approximately one third of the total weight to a single ordinal criterion,
reaction to fire, on which mineral-based products separate sharply from the
rest; it therefore places mineral wools first. CRITIC and equal weighting place
the same bio-based alternatives at the top. The finding persists on the subset
backed by verified product declarations. Agreement between TOPSIS and VIKOR is
0.350–0.509, indicating that the ranking method also shapes the outcome.

**Result 3 — the system boundary changes the winner.** Product declaration
modules show that the biogenic carbon taken up by bio-based materials in the
production stage is released again at end of life; for six materials, uptake and
release cancel to within 0.04 kgCO₂e/kg. Truncating the account at the factory
gate makes bio-based materials appear carbon-negative and places them first;
including end-of-life modules moves glass wool to first place in every zone. The
effect strengthens when two records with inconsistent data versions are removed
and holds under all three weighting methods. Defining the realised release
fraction as a sensitivity parameter shows that a bio-based material remains
first until more than half of the stored carbon is released.

**Conclusion.** The three results converge: a material ranking is interpretable
only when the framing that produced it — functional unit, weighting method,
ranking method and system boundary — is fully declared. These four are as much
part of the result as the result itself. The rankings reported here are
indicative, constrained by data quality; the structural findings are independent
of it.
